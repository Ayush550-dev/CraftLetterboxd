A

Ajrakh

Traditional Craft
★★★★★ 5/5 Highly complex due to multiple printing, resist and dyeing stages.
Genre
Resist printing / Natural dyeing
Origin
Kutch, Gujarat; also practised in Barmer, Rajasthan and historically associated with Sindh.
Starring / Cast
Indigo, madder red, black, white, geometric patterns, wooden blocks and natural dyes.
Director
Khatri artisan communities.

Plot

Ajrakh is a traditional hand-block-printing technique involving an elaborate sequence of washing, mordanting, resist printing and dyeing. The fabric is printed on both sides, often using intricate geometric patterns. Its production can involve more than twenty stages, with the fabric being repeatedly washed and dried between processes. Indigo and madder are particularly characteristic colours.

Review

Ajrakh's geometric repetition and strong colour contrasts could translate beautifully into couture. It could be used for structured jackets, corset panels, dramatic sleeves or layered garments where the pattern becomes part of the silhouette rather than simply surface decoration.

Aari Embroidery

Technique
★★★★★ 4/5 Requires precision and consistent stitch control.
Genre
Hook embroidery / Surface embellishment
Origin
Kashmir, with related traditions practised in Gujarat and Rajasthan.
Starring / Cast
Aari needle, silk thread, cotton, metallic thread, floral motifs and chain stitches.
Director
Traditional Kashmiri and regional embroidery artisans.

Plot

Aari embroidery is worked using a hooked needle called an aari. The hook pulls thread through the fabric to create continuous chain stitches. It allows artisans to produce elaborate floral and paisley patterns at a relatively rapid pace. Depending on the regional style, it may incorporate beads, sequins, metallic threads and other embellishments.

Review

Aari embroidery could be adapted into couture through oversized floral motifs, heavily embroidered sleeves, raised surface textures and sculptural embellishment. Its continuous chain stitch also makes it suitable for creating linear patterns that follow garment seams.

Appliqué Work (Pipli)

Traditional Craft
★★★★★ 3/5 The basic process is accessible, although complex compositions require careful cutting and placement.
Genre
Fabric manipulation / Surface decoration
Origin
Pipili, Odisha.
Starring / Cast
Coloured cotton fabric, contrasting fabric pieces, thread, mirrors, geometric shapes and floral motifs.
Director
Pipili appliqué artisan communities.

Plot

Pipili appliqué is a traditional craft in which differently coloured pieces of fabric are cut into shapes and stitched onto a larger fabric base. The shapes may include flowers, animals, birds, celestial forms and geometric designs. Traditionally used for temple canopies, umbrellas, ceremonial decorations and festival hangings, the craft is recognised for its bold contrasts and layered construction.

Review

This technique has excellent couture potential because it creates dimension without relying entirely on embroidery. Fabric pieces could be layered, enlarged or cut into abstract forms to create sculptural garments, detachable panels and exaggerated silhouettes.

B

Bandhani

Traditional Craft
★★★★★ 5/5 Extremely time-consuming when thousands of individual knots are required.
Genre
Resist dyeing / Tie-dye
Origin
Gujarat and Rajasthan.
Starring / Cast
Cotton, silk, threads, tiny tied knots, dots, circles and vibrant dyes.
Director
Bandhani artisan communities, particularly Khatri dyers.

Plot

Bandhani is a traditional resist-dyeing technique in which small sections of fabric are pinched and tightly tied with thread before being immersed in dye. The tied portions resist the dye, creating characteristic patterns of small circles and dots. Different tying arrangements create patterns such as circles, waves and intricate floral compositions. The process can be repeated with multiple colours to achieve layered designs.

Review

Bandhani could be explored through controlled gathering, pleating and surface manipulation. The tied areas could also inspire three-dimensional garment details, clustered fabric knots and textured bodices.

Bagru Printing

Traditional Craft
★★★★★ 4/5 Requires accuracy in block placement, resist application and dyeing.
Genre
Hand-block printing / Resist printing
Origin
Bagru, Rajasthan.
Starring / Cast
Wooden blocks, natural dyes, indigo, red, black, mud paste and floral or geometric motifs.
Director
Chhipa printing communities.

Plot

Bagru printing is a traditional hand-block-printing practice in which carved wooden blocks are used to transfer patterns onto fabric. One of its important techniques is Dabu, a mud-resist process in which a paste made using clay and other materials is applied to selected areas before dyeing. This protects those sections from absorbing the dye, producing patterned contrasts. Traditional Bagru textiles often feature earthy colours and repeated floral or geometric motifs.

Review

The technique could be used in couture through oversized printed panels, contrasting borders and repeated motifs. The mud-resist process also presents possibilities for experimenting with unconventional resist materials and developing contemporary surface patterns.

Baluchari Weaving

Regional Textile
★★★★★ 5/5 Detailed figured weaving requires considerable skill and planning.
Genre
Silk weaving / Figured brocade
Origin
Murshidabad historically; now particularly associated with Bishnupur, West Bengal.
Starring / Cast
Silk yarn, supplementary weft, zari, mythological scenes, architectural motifs and elaborate borders.
Director
Traditional Baluchari weaving communities.

Plot

Baluchari is a richly patterned silk textile traditionally recognised for its elaborate pallu, which often depicts scenes from Indian mythology, epics and historical or courtly life. The motifs are woven into the fabric using supplementary weft techniques, allowing intricate pictorial compositions to become part of the textile itself rather than being printed or embroidered onto it.

Review

Baluchari's narrative quality could be explored in couture through garments that tell stories across their panels. The woven scenes could be enlarged, fragmented or repositioned to create a relationship between textile storytelling and garment construction.

Review

Chikankari could be adapted into contemporary couture through sheer layered garments, translucent sleeves, embroidered cut-outs and sculptural white-on-white surfaces. Its delicate nature could create an interesting contrast with exaggerated garment structures.

Chamba Rumal

Traditional Craft
★★★★★ 5/5 Requires detailed drawing, careful counting and precise double-sided embroidery.
Genre
Double-sided embroidery / Narrative needlework
Origin
Chamba, Himachal Pradesh.
Starring / Cast
Untwisted silk threads, cotton fabric, miniature-inspired figures, floral motifs and double satin stitches.
Director

Plot

Chamba Rumal is a traditional embroidered textile known for its detailed narrative compositions. It frequently depicts religious scenes, particularly episodes from Krishna's life, alongside courtly and floral imagery. Its distinguishing feature is the dorukha stitch, a double-sided satin stitch that produces a similar appearance on both sides of the fabric. The designs were historically influenced by Pahari miniature painting.

Review

The double-sided quality could be explored through reversible couture garments, transparent layers and embroidered panels visible from both sides. The narrative motifs could also be reinterpreted as contemporary illustrations across a garment.

D

Dabu Printing

Technique
★★★★★ 4/5 The preparation of the resist paste and control of dye penetration require experience.
Genre
Mud-resist printing / Hand-block printing
Origin
Rajasthan, particularly Bagru and surrounding regions.
Starring / Cast
Clay, gum, lime, wooden blocks, sawdust, indigo and cotton fabric.
Director
Traditional Chhipa printing communities.

Plot

Dabu is a resist-printing technique in which a specially prepared mud paste is applied to selected sections of fabric using carved wooden blocks. The printed fabric may be sprinkled with sawdust to prevent the resist from sticking to itself. It is then dyed, commonly in indigo, with the protected areas retaining their original colour. The process can be repeated to create layered patterns.

Review

Dabu could inspire contemporary couture through negative-space printing, irregular patterns and contrasting surface treatments. Experimenting with different resist materials could also help develop new textures and effects.

Dhaka Muslin

Regional Textile
★★★★★ 5/5 Producing exceptionally fine yarn and weaving it consistently is extremely demanding.
Genre
Fine cotton weaving / Muslin
Origin
Historically associated with Dhaka, in present-day Bangladesh, and the wider Bengal region.
Starring / Cast
Exceptionally fine cotton yarn, lightweight fibres, delicate weave structures and translucent surfaces.
Director
Historical Bengal muslin weavers.

Plot

Dhaka muslin was historically celebrated for its exceptionally fine cotton yarn and lightweight construction. It was woven using specialised cotton fibres and skilled hand-spinning and weaving techniques. The resulting fabric was famously delicate, soft and almost transparent. Its production declined significantly under colonial economic conditions, making historical muslin an important example of a highly developed textile tradition that was disrupted.

Review

Its relationship with transparency could inspire couture through delicate layered dresses, sheer panels and garments that explore the relationship between concealment and visibility. Contemporary versions could also explore how fine fabrics interact with structured underlayers.

Kota Doria is a lightweight handwoven textile recognised for its distinctive square-check pattern, known as khat. The structure is created through the arrangement of cotton and silk yarns in the warp and weft. The resulting fabric is airy, transparent and lightweight while retaining a recognisable grid-like structure.

Review

The checked structure could inspire couture through geometric panel placement, layered transparent fabrics and exaggerated grid constructions. The textile could also be used to create volume while maintaining lightness.

Draping

Technique
★★★★★ 5/5 Requires a strong understanding of fabric behaviour, proportion, balance and three-dimensional construction.
Genre
Three-dimensional garment construction
Origin
Used across historical clothing traditions; developed into a significant couture design and construction method in twentieth-century Paris.
Starring / Cast
Muslin, silk, satin, organza, jersey, dress forms, pins and fabric weights.
Director
Madeleine Vionnet and contemporary couture drapers.

Plot

Draping is a garment construction technique in which fabric is arranged, folded, pinned and manipulated directly on a dress form or body to develop a garment's shape. Unlike flat pattern drafting, it allows designers to explore volume, movement, asymmetry and sculptural forms in three dimensions. The fabric is adjusted directly on the form until the desired silhouette is achieved, after which it can be transferred into a pattern.

Review

Draping offers endless possibilities for couture because it allows fabric to determine the form of a garment. It could be explored through exaggerated folds, cascading fabric, sculptural shoulders, asymmetrical gowns and garments that appear to grow organically around the body.

E

Eri Silk

Material
★★★★★ 4/5 Fibre processing and yarn preparation require specialised knowledge.
Genre
Silk production / Handloom weaving
Origin
Assam and other parts of Northeast India.
Starring / Cast
Eri silkworm cocoons, spun silk yarn, natural ivory and beige shades, soft textured fabric.
Director
Traditional Eri silk-rearing and weaving communities.

Plot

Eri silk is produced from the cocoons of the Samia ricini silkworm. Unlike conventional mulberry silk, the cocoon is generally processed after the moth has emerged, leaving an opening. The resulting fibre is spun into yarn and woven into soft, warm textiles. Eri is sometimes called peace silk because of this method of obtaining the fibre, although production practices can vary.

Review

Its natural texture and soft drape could be explored in contemporary couture through oversized outerwear, layered garments and textured surfaces. It is also relevant for designers interested in alternative silk production and material innovation.

Embroidery (Indian Traditional Embroidery)

Technique
★★★★★ 4/5 Difficulty varies considerably depending on the embroidery tradition.
Genre
Surface embellishment / Needlework
Origin
Practised throughout India, with distinctive regional traditions.
Starring / Cast
Needles, silk, cotton, metallic threads, beads, mirrors, sequins and a wide range of regional stitches.
Director
Regional embroidery artisan communities.

Plot

Embroidery is the process of decorating a textile using needle and thread. India has an extensive range of regional embroidery traditions, including Phulkari, Chikankari, Kantha, Kasuti, Kashida, Zardozi and Toda embroidery. These techniques differ in their stitches, materials, motifs and cultural functions. Some are used for everyday clothing, while others are associated with ceremonial and royal garments.

Review

Embroidery offers almost unlimited couture possibilities. It can be used to create raised textures, three-dimensional surfaces, sculptural embellishments and narrative compositions. Rather than treating it as decoration added at the end, embroidery could be integrated into the actual construction of a garment.

Entoilage

Technique
★★★★★ 4/5 Requires knowledge of fabric compatibility, reinforcement materials, placement and appropriate application methods.
Genre
Internal garment reinforcement and construction
Origin
French dressmaking and couture construction traditions.
Starring / Cast
Fusible and sew-in interfacings, canvas, organza, cotton, hair canvas, silk and supporting fabrics.
Director
French couture ateliers and traditional tailors.

Plot

Entoilage is a French dressmaking term referring to the application of an interlining or reinforcing material to specific sections of a garment. It is used to provide additional body, stability and structure to fabrics that might otherwise collapse or lose their shape. Commonly applied to collars, cuffs, waistbands, facings and other areas requiring support, it plays an important role in achieving a clean and controlled garment finish.

Review

Entoilage is particularly interesting because much of its work remains hidden within the garment. It could be explored through contrasting levels of stiffness, experimenting with different supporting materials and creating sculptural silhouettes that hold their shape without relying entirely on visible embellishment.

F

Fluting

Technique
★★★★★ 4/5 Requires control over fabric behaviour, consistent folds and appropriate shaping or stiffening methods to maintain the desired structure.
Genre
Three-dimensional fabric manipulation and surface construction
Origin
European decorative dressmaking and textile traditions, with contemporary applications in couture.
Starring / Cast
Silk, satin, organza, tulle, chiffon, horsehair braid, stiffened fabric and heat-set synthetic textiles.
Director
Traditional dressmakers and contemporary couture designers.

Plot

Fluting is a fabric manipulation technique that creates repeated wave-like ridges or undulating folds across a textile surface. The effect can be achieved through controlled folding, gathering, pleating, shaping or stiffening techniques, depending on the fabric. Fluted surfaces introduce movement, volume and texture while allowing the fabric to take on a more sculptural appearance.

Review

Fluting could be translated into couture through exaggerated sleeves, sculptural collars, dimensional skirts and layered bodice details. It could also be experimented with using heat-setting, fabric stiffeners and unconventional materials to create surfaces that resemble waves, shells or organic structures.

I

Ikat

Technique
★★★★★ 5/5 Yarn must be tied, dyed and aligned accurately before weaving.
Genre
Resist dyeing / Patterned weaving
Origin
Gujarat, Odisha, Telangana and Andhra Pradesh, with distinct regional traditions.
Starring / Cast
Cotton, silk, resist-dyed yarns, geometric patterns and blurred motifs.
Director
Traditional ikat weaving communities.

Plot

Ikat is a textile technique in which yarns are tied and dyed in selected sections before being woven into fabric. The resist-dyeing process determines where colour appears in the finished textile. Depending on whether the warp, weft or both are dyed, ikat is classified as warp ikat, weft ikat or double ikat. The slightly blurred appearance of the motifs is one of its most recognisable features. Indian examples include Pochampally, Sambalpuri and Patola.

Review

Ikat could be translated into couture through distorted geometric prints, colour transitions and panelled garments. The blurred quality could also inspire layered appliqué or printed surfaces that imitate the appearance of woven resist-dyed patterns.

Indigo Dyeing

Technique
★★★★★ 4/5 Maintaining the dye bath and controlling colour consistency require experience.
Genre
Natural dyeing / Resist and immersion dyeing
Origin
Practised in several Indian textile centres, including Gujarat, Rajasthan and parts of South India.
Starring / Cast
Indigofera plants, indigo pigment, cotton, silk, dye vats and shades of blue.
Director
Traditional natural dyers and textile artisans.

Plot

Indigo dyeing is a traditional method of colouring textiles using indigo pigment. Unlike many other dyes, indigo does not initially dissolve in water, so it must be converted into a soluble form through a reduction process. The fabric is dipped into the dye bath and develops its characteristic blue colour when exposed to air. Multiple dips create deeper shades. Indigo is particularly important in traditions such as Ajrakh.

Review

Indigo could be used in couture through tonal layering, dip-dye effects, gradient surfaces and combinations of different blue shades. It could also be paired with resist techniques to develop complex patterns.

Review

Jamdani could be adapted into couture through strategically placed motifs, sheer overlays and garments that use the relationship between solid pattern and transparent ground. Its floating motifs could also inspire three-dimensional embellishment.

K

Kalamkari

Traditional Craft
★★★★★ 5/5 Multiple stages and detailed drawing or printing require specialised skills.
Genre
Hand painting / Block printing / Natural dyeing
Origin
Andhra Pradesh, particularly Srikalahasti and Machilipatnam.
Starring / Cast
Kalam (pen), wooden blocks, cotton, natural dyes, mythological figures, floral and ornamental motifs.
Director
Kalamkari artisan communities.

Plot

Kalamkari is a traditional Indian textile art involving the application of designs onto fabric using natural dyes. It has two major styles: Srikalahasti, which is primarily hand-drawn using a pen-like instrument called a kalam, and Machilipatnam, which uses carved wooden blocks for printing. Traditional themes include mythology, temple imagery, flowers and decorative patterns. The process involves several stages of washing, mordanting, drawing and dyeing.

Review

Kalamkari could be used in couture as a storytelling surface. Its narrative compositions could be fragmented across garment panels, enlarged into statement prints or combined with embroidery to create garments that communicate through imagery.

Kantha

Origin
West Bengal and Bangladesh.
Starring / Cast
Recycled cotton sarees, old cloth, coloured threads, running stitches and narrative motifs.

Traditional Bengali women embroiderers.

Plot

Weaving is the process of interlacing two sets of yarns, known as warp and weft, at right angles to create fabric. Indian weaving traditions include Jamdani, Patola, Banarasi brocade, Paithani, Kanjeevaram, Chanderi and many others. Review
